Music Guides

Guides on how albums are put together, how to remember them, and what to listen for. Each one uses real albums from the game as examples.

  • Why Album Sequencing Matters: Five Real Examples

    The same songs in a different order make a different album. Five well-known records show how track order shapes pacing, story and endings.

  • How to Remember an Album's Tracklist: Seven Methods That Work

    Memorizing the order of an album is a skill, not a talent. Seven practical techniques, with worked examples from The Beatles, Beyoncé and more.

  • Albums vs. Playlists: What You Gain and Lose With Each

    Albums give you an arc chosen by the artist, playlists give you control. A balanced look at both, with real examples from Beyoncé, Frank Ocean and The Beatles.

  • Why You Remember Song Order Without Trying

    Why can you predict the next song on an album you haven't heard in years? A look at how memory for music works, and what it means for music trivia.

  • Exploring Genres Through Albums: Eight Starting Points

    A practical way into unfamiliar genres is to start with one well-built album. Eight starting points across pop, hip-hop, R&B, Latin, K-pop, rock and more.

  • Skits, Interludes and Intros: Why They Make Albums Harder to Memorize

    Short spoken tracks and interludes are the hardest part of an album to remember. A look at why artists use them, with counts from Eminem, Kanye West, Drake and BTS.

  • From 4 Tracks to 27: How Album Length Has Changed

    Album lengths range from 4 tracks to 27. A look at why length has changed, how technology shaped it, and which tracklists are easiest and hardest to learn.

  • How Albums Start and End: The Art of the Opener and the Closer

    The first and last tracks are the most carefully chosen on any album. Common strategies for openers and closers, with real examples from The Beatles, Kanye West, Frank Ocean and more.

  • The Beatles' Tracklists From Please Please Me to Let It Be

    Reading the Beatles' albums in order shows how pop records changed in seven years. Track counts, closers and structure from 1963 to 1970, plus why version differences matter.

  • Concept Albums and Story Arcs: Albums Built to Be Heard in Order

    Some albums are designed so that the order is part of the meaning. Six signs an album is built to be heard in sequence, with examples from The Beatles, Beyoncé, BTS and others.
