The albums covered in this guide run from 4 tracks to 27. That's not just a difference in quantity. A four-track release is almost a single idea, and a 27-track album is closer to a small library. Looking at the numbers across the catalog tells an interesting story about how technology, business models and artistic ambition have changed what an album is.

## How technology set the limits

For most of the 20th century, an album's length was decided by a physical object. A vinyl LP holds somewhere around 20 minutes per side if you want good sound quality, which is why classic albums tend to be between 35 and 50 minutes. That limit encouraged tightness: artists had to choose what made the cut.

The compact disc, which became standard in the 1980s and 1990s, raised the ceiling to around 74 to 80 minutes. Many artists filled it, and some of the long, uneven albums of the CD era were the result. Then streaming removed the ceiling entirely. A digital album can be any length, and some now run well over an hour and a half.

## The early Beatles: 13 to 14 tracks

The Beatles' early albums are a good picture of what the vinyl era produced. In the UK versions in our game, *Please Please Me* (1963), *With the Beatles* (1963), *Beatles for Sale* (1964), *Help!* (1965), *Rubber Soul* (1965) and *Revolver* (1966) all have 14 tracks, and *A Hard Day's Night* (1964) has 13. *Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band* (1967) also has 13. The pattern is very consistent: about 14 songs, divided across two sides, in under 40 minutes.

There were exceptions even then. *The Beatles*, the double album known as the White Album (1968), runs to 30 tracks, and *Abbey Road* (1969) reaches 17 with its medley of short pieces.

## The streaming era: 20 tracks and up

The albums at the long end of our catalog are almost all recent.

- **Cowboy Carter** (Beyoncé, 2024): 27 tracks
- **Scorpion** (Drake, 2018): 25 tracks
- **Un Verano Sin Ti** (Bad Bunny, 2022): 23 tracks
- **For All the Dogs** (Drake, 2023): 23 tracks
- **Nadie Sabe Lo Que Va A Pasar Mañana** (Bad Bunny, 2023): 22 tracks
- **More Life** (Drake, 2017): 22 tracks
- **Certified Lover Boy** (Drake, 2021): 21 tracks

Several things push in this direction. The Billboard albums chart began counting streams as part of its calculation in the mid-2010s, and critics have argued that this rewards longer tracklists, because more tracks mean more chances to be streamed. Artists also no longer need to worry about physical limits, and fans of big artists are happy to get more music. And some long albums are made on purpose to feel like a mixtape or a whole world, not just a set of songs.

It's worth noting that length isn't automatically a flaw. *Un Verano Sin Ti* is 23 tracks long and holds together because it keeps a consistent mood. *Scorpion* is divided into two clear halves, with the first mostly rap and the second mostly R&B, which gives its 25 tracks a structure you can learn.

## The counter-trend: short and sharp

At the other end are the albums that go short on purpose.

- **Yeezus** (Kanye West, 2013): 10 tracks
- **4 Your Eyez Only** (J. Cole, 2016): 10 tracks
- **Hit Me Hard and Soft** (Billie Eilish, 2024): 10 tracks
- **B'Day** (Beyoncé, 2006): 10 tracks
- **Born Pink** (BLACKPINK, 2022): 8 tracks
- **Be** (BTS, 2020): 8 tracks
- **Don't Smile at Me** (Billie Eilish, 2017): 8 tracks
- **Square Up** (BLACKPINK, 2018): 4 tracks
- **Amortage** (Jisoo, 2025): 4 tracks

Some of these are formally EPs or mini-albums, and some are full albums by choice. They share a few traits: little filler, a consistent sound, and a running time that doesn't ask much of the listener. In a time when the average listener's attention is divided, a tight 30 to 40 minutes can feel like a relief.

## What length means for memory

Album length matters a lot for anyone trying to learn a tracklist, and the effect is not linear. Memory researchers have long noted that people can hold only a small number of items in working memory at once, a limit often summarized as around seven, give or take a couple. That's a rough figure and still debated, but the practical lesson is clear: a four-track release fits in your head whole, and a 27-track album can't.

A few consequences:

**Short albums are nearly all anchor.** With four or eight tracks, almost every position is close to the beginning or end, and those are the positions people remember best.

**Medium albums (10 to 14 tracks) are the sweet spot for the game.** They're long enough to be challenging and short enough that you can chunk them into two or three groups.

**Long albums need chunks.** You can't memorize 25 titles in a row, but you can memorize five groups of five. Finding the natural groupings, such as sides, moods or collaborators, is the whole game.

**Very long albums reward structure.** *Cowboy Carter*'s interludes and *Scorpion*'s two-sided design give you landmarks. Albums without those landmarks are much harder.

## Which should you try first?

If you're new to the game, start with something short. Try [Yeezus](/albums/kanye-west-yeezus.html), [Hit Me Hard and Soft](/albums/billie-eilish-hit-me-hard-and-soft.html) or [Born Pink](/albums/blackpink-born-pink.html), where every track matters and there are only a few places to go wrong.

When you're ready for a challenge, step up to something in the 20s. [Scorpion](/albums/drake-scorpion.html) is easier than its length suggests because of its two-sided structure, and [Un Verano Sin Ti](/albums/bad-bunny-un-verano-sin-ti.html) is a good test of whether you know a long album as a whole.

Afterwards, check what you got wrong. The mistakes are rarely random. They're almost always on tracks in the middle, which is where every long album is hardest to remember.
